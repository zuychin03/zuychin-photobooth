# P4 motion export contract

Implemented locally on 23/09/2026. `lib/exports/motion.ts` exports the encoder/capture APIs. Callers provide already-composed PNG/JPEG frames, own the download UI, abort on navigation, and retain their original still projects. Motion frames and outputs are ephemeral; this module never writes IndexedDB, uploads media, changes the project manifest or requests camera/microphone access.

## APIs and bounds

- `encodeMotionGif(frames, options)` returns an actual GIF artifact. Defaults are four requested frames per second and a maximum 640-pixel edge, fitting the original aspect ratio. `maxEdge` may lower that bound. `boomerang` appends the reversed interior frames without duplicating the endpoints. A four-photo loop therefore becomes six frames; 24 captured frames become 46.
- `encodeMotionVideo(frames, {format, ...options})` accepts explicit `mp4` or `webm`. It fits frames within 1280 × 720, letterboxes differing frame proportions, and records only a private canvas stream without audio. An artifact is returned only after requested/recorder/Blob MIME and container agreement, native decode at the expected dimensions, and playback to completion. It never relabels WebM as MP4.
- `captureSoloFrames(video, options)` is called only after the user explicitly starts a short solo capture. It borrows the existing video, uses its current pixels with optional mirror and a known built-in `filterId`, and never starts or stops the user's camera tracks. Defaults are two seconds and a requested 12 fps. An unsupported canvas filter is reported in `warnings`; it is never claimed as applied.
- `probeMotionVideoFormats(signal?)` performs actual synthetic encoding and native playback for each advertised format. `available` becomes true only after this passes. A missing advertised candidate is `unsupported`; an advertised candidate that fails real encode/decode is `fail`. Capability is an observation of this browser session, not a cross-device promise or guarantee that a later recording will succeed.
- `runMotionProbe(signal?)` is a development-only synthetic worker/native decoder probe plus video capability checks. It requests no camera or microphone.

`MotionArtifact` includes the actual Blob/MIME/extension/bytes, width/height, supplied frame count, encoded or decoded duration, elapsed work time, requested frame rate and warnings. Video adds decoded sample counts. These counts do not establish the encoded video frame rate. GIF frame delays are rounded to its 10 ms time unit and the returned duration uses those encoded delays. MP4/WebM duration uses completed native playback; browser metadata may otherwise report infinity. Frame proportions are preserved, with at most the necessary even-pixel rounding and letterboxing for video compatibility.

All encoders accept 2–48 input PNG/JPEG frames, at most 10 MiB total encoded input, at most 48 output frames after boomerang and at most eight seconds of requested playback. Frame delays correspond to 0.5–12 requested fps. GIF output uses a 256-colour local palette per frame and may show photo colour banding; no dithering or perceptual quality guarantee is claimed. Output is capped at 10 MiB. A 24-frame, 12 fps camera boomerang is approximately 3.83 seconds before GIF delay rounding, rather than another recording period.

Solo capture stores at most 24 sequentially encoded JPEG Blobs within 10 MiB. It begins each capture within a two-second sampling window and reports actual elapsed work and actual captured frame count. Encoding that finishes after the last sampling instant may extend elapsed work. Slow encodes skip the missed schedule and wait for the next opportunity; there is no catch-up burst or claim of a measured 12 fps. Fewer than two captured frames rejects the loop honestly. Camera capture failure does not affect existing still photos.

## Decode, worker and lifecycle boundaries

Before native decode, every input passes the existing still-image header parser: declared MIME must match, dimensions are bounded to 4096 per edge and 12 MiPixels, and animated/unsupported formats are rejected. Each native bitmap is decoded and closed sequentially. A late bitmap after timeout/cancellation is immediately closed. The input queue consists of compressed Blobs, never 24 retained raw RGBA surfaces.

Transparent PNG frames are deliberately composited onto white before GIF quantisation or video recording. The output is opaque. Different frame proportions fit within the first frame's bounded dimensions with white letterboxing, so exported width/height always describe the encoded canvas rather than a stretched source.

A shared motion-operation guard rejects overlapping capture/GIF/video calls. It releases after success, failure or cancellation. Native bitmap decoding has an additional guard retained until the underlying decoder actually settles, including after an outward timeout. Rapid retries cannot stack abandoned native decodes. If a native decoder never settles, further motion decode attempts report that pending state until reload; existing still media remain available.

The GIF worker is created only when GIF export starts. Its module imports the pinned `gifenc@1.0.3`. Main-thread decoding/rasterisation transfers one RGBA buffer, then waits for that frame's acknowledgement before decoding another. The worker validates dimensions/count/order, quantises the current frame, appends to one bounded GIF stream and releases frame-local state. Cancellation, worker failure and job timeout terminate the worker in `finally`; there is no main-thread fallback that freezes the UI. The main thread retains one current bitmap and one bounded drawing canvas, plus one transferred frame being encoded. GIF internal growable buffers, compression tables, native decoder/GPU memory and copied output are additional transient allocations, not a total-process-memory guarantee.

Video recording bounds chunk accumulation and has a ten-second recording stop limit. Default end-to-end export budget is 40 seconds, including native playback verification; GIF defaults to 30 seconds. Every outcome stops only the private canvas stream's tracks, stops the recorder, removes event handlers, clears timers, revokes the playback URL, removes the temporary video, and zeros temporary canvases. Borrowed camera streams and input Blobs remain untouched. Synchronous native work cannot be forcibly interrupted; late results are discarded and released. Keep the tab visible because background throttling changes timing.

Held frames are periodically redrawn/requested at no more than 12 times per second, including the final hold. This prevents change-driven canvas streams from dropping the intended last-frame duration. A final one-frame settling interval precedes recorder stop, and native playback that is over 250 ms shorter than the requested sequence rejects the output. Actual duration is still measured rather than claimed as exact scheduling.

## Evidence and acceptance

Node tests exercise real GIF encoding, parse the complete frame blocks and LZW-decode fixture pixels, verify local palettes/delays/order, exercise queue/source-byte/geometry limits, test slow capture scheduling and cancellation, and prove worker termination/bitmap cleanup after timeout. These are not native browser playback results.

The development probe builds four synthetic native PNGs, runs the actual worker, and, where native `ImageDecoder` exists, decodes every frame of the six-frame boomerang and verifies dimensions and expected colours. Missing `ImageDecoder` is reported as unsupported multi-frame verification, not a failed GIF format or a claim of tested playback. The video probe performs real encode/decode and requires changing sampled pixels and a bounded measured duration.

Before that successful GIF encode, the native probe aborts a first worker job after its first progress acknowledgement, verifies that no second progress event completed and that all original PNG hashes are unchanged, then starts a new encoder. The retry must produce all six frames and is the artifact sent through the native multi-frame decoder. This separates mid-encode cancellation/recovery from merely rejecting an already-aborted request.

Root records browser evidence separately. Real camera capture, iPhone Safari/PWA, Android, long-session/native-memory observation and user download/playback remain acceptance checks. An API advertisement, GIF signature or Node test alone does not pass them. There is no FFmpeg dependency, MP4 conversion service, microphone track, remote/group motion or hidden continuous recording.
